# Researching public H-1B and employer information

Public data can help you form follow-up questions. It cannot establish that a live role will sponsor a visa, that an employer will select a candidate, or that a candidate is eligible.

## A practical order of checks

1. Start with the employer's official careers page: job title, requisition ID, location, application status, and posting/update dates.
2. Search the [DOL Office of Foreign Labor Certification disclosure data](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/foreign-labor/performance) by the employer's legal name and the relevant reporting period. Use the record layout to understand fields and dates.
3. If STEM OPT is relevant, use the official [E-Verify Employer Search](https://www.e-verify.gov/e-verify-employer-search) and confirm the legal entity and E-Verify details directly with the employer and DSO.
4. Keep each conclusion separate: historical filing data, current job language, employer confirmation, and individual eligibility are different facts.

## Data limitations

- DOL disclosure files cover reported LCA-related data and the stated reporting period. They are not a live vacancy list or a current sponsor commitment.
- The E-Verify search tool may use legal names, omit some locations, and does not by itself resolve whether a particular job qualifies. Review USCIS's [official caveats](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/USCIS/Verification/E-Verify/About%20the%20Program/Search%20Tools/E-VerifyEmployerSearchToolCaveats.pdf).
- The public E-Verify endpoint was not tested with an employer query in this preparation run. Check its current availability and the employer's legal name at the time of use; do not use a mirror or third-party copy as a substitute.
- USCIS's H-1B Employer Data Hub provides historical petition information. Its current availability was not verified in this preparation run; this guide uses DOL's verified disclosure-data entry above as its primary research route.
